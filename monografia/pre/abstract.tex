   This work investigates whether NLP-derived sentiment and event features are capable of improving the risk-adjusted performance of a Forex trading strategy beyond a price-only baseline. The question matters because the Forex market is strongly driven by news flow and macroeconomic information, yet the literature on text-based prediction of financial returns has concentrated on equities, leaving the asset class comparatively underexplored. The proposed approach builds, on two major Forex pairs at minute resolution, a hybrid trading pipeline that combines technical signals with sentiment and event-based features extracted from financial text and from a public geopolitical-risk index, fused into a single trade decision under strict point-in-time discipline and a conservative publication-to-use delay; the precise sources, scorers and decision components are detailed in the methodology. Evaluation contrasts the hybrid against the price-only baseline out of sample under realistic transaction costs, complemented by an ablation that isolates the marginal contribution of the textual channel and by a formal statistical test of the difference in return distributions. The contribution is twofold: an empirical, falsifiable test of the hypothesis that adding NLP-derived textual features improves risk-adjusted performance on the two target pairs, and a reusable methodology that subsequent work can extend without reimplementation.
