\chapter{Conclusion} \label{cap:conclusion}

\section{Final Remarks} \label{sec:final-remarks}

This work develops an architecture for combining price-derived and news/event features in a deterministic Forex execution system. The implementation has reached offline baseline/hybrid training and a fixed-model EUR/USD pilot, as documented in Chapter~\ref{cap:experimental-evaluation}. The ten-year, multi-source, rolling/CPCV evaluation remains a target rather than a completed empirical result.

The evidence currently supports the feasibility of the data, model-persistence, and replay path. It does not establish or reject hypothesis~H1. Zero trades, a nonsignificant diagnostic, or missing paired results cannot demonstrate that news has no value. Conversely, a favorable pilot return would not establish generalization without coverage, cost, leakage, and statistical checks.

At the September 28, 2026, 00:36 UTC snapshot, the completed execution-model replays in Chapter~\ref{cap:experimental-evaluation} record losses exceeding 50\% for both strategies in September and October 2015. Other exploratory variants also lose money or place no trades. The annual-model diagnostics show F7 probabilities opposing the trend regime on average, but this descriptive anti-alignment is neither a demonstrated cause of the losses nor evidence that reversing signals would be profitable. Both March--October 2016 simulations have since completed with successful final manifests and lose about 88\% of the deposit each (Section~\ref{sec:confirmation-results}); the news family changes the outcome by about \$65 over 1,029 trades. That untouched-window result is the strongest evidence in this work, and it is negative for the pilot models under the registered execution model. Fifty-one further tuning variants on the H4, H1 and M15 clocks with the TA-Lib detector, the quote-activity gate, calibrated thresholds, alternative plans and stops (Section~\ref{subsec:tuning-sweeps}) confirm it: no variant is profitable in November 2016, the one month untouched by any decision, and the loss grows with the number of trades at every timeframe.

Every reported run is linked to its archived F1--F7 parameters and model identity, with missing historical settings explicitly marked. The SpockFX-derived M1 sweeps do not reproduce the reference H1/H4 system. Repeated threshold, gate, execution, and strategy selection prevents treating the reused pilot as confirmatory out-of-sample evidence. These intermediate observations motivate further diagnosis and a declared evaluation protocol; they do not establish an economically validated strategy or a news contribution.

The separate 01:11 UTC H4 snapshot completes all eight baseline/hybrid candle-by-quote-activity cells. Each volume-off cell records six closed trades and a 1.36\% equity return; each volume-on cell records three and 2.87\%, with 5.1\% engine-reported maximum drawdown throughout. Only 51 decisions remain after 59 warm-up bars. Active TA-Lib patterns and event-augmented probabilities do not cross the fixed F7 decision boundary, so identical paired outcomes establish neither a disconnected feature path nor a general absence of news or pattern value. The interrupted ENOSPC hybrid-v1 attempt remains separately visible. These H4 swing proxies are not the original SpockFX system, and repeated exploration of September prevents promoting the positive results to confirmatory out-of-sample evidence.

The workflow in Section~\ref{subsec:workflow-stages} makes the remaining requirements explicit: complete and audit the broader paired replay, validate execution assumptions, supply audited equity, cost and selection-history inputs to the corrected DSR and paired time-series inference of Section~\ref{subsec:rule-significance}, and extend evaluation under a declared protocol. TA-Lib pattern detection is now observed in the H4 experiment; text sentiment, GPR evidence, and broader validation remain unestablished. Any item unfinished at submission must remain an explicit limitation or deferred task, rather than being counted as an achieved methodological contribution.

\section{Future Work} \label{sec:future-work}

The methodology of Chapter~\ref{cap:methodology} is the foundation for the subsequent research directions discussed in this section. The unifying objective behind them is to take the minimum viable product targeted by the present work to a production-grade algorithmic-trading system suitable for live capital deployment. Tiers A to D below organize the evolution of the perception layer; Section~\ref{subsec:future-platform} discusses the eventual migration of the execution engine; Section~\ref{subsec:future-live} discusses the transition from backtest to paper trading to live trading; the remaining subsections extend the empirical evaluation rather than the methodology. The deterministic execution layer of Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution} and the YAML-configurable filter chain are intended to make each extension additive: a configuration change or a new perception adapter rather than an architectural rewrite.

\subsection{Tier A: richer single-agent perception} \label{subsec:future-tier-a}

The simplest extension replaces the FinBERT-class scorer of Section~\ref{subsec:sentiment-features} with a more capable language-model agent. A structured-output prompt against a contemporary foundation model returns, per article, a polarity score, a confidence score, and a short reasoning trace. The reasoning trace is consumed only for auditing and post-hoc analysis; the scalar polarity and confidence enter the feature stream just as FinBERT outputs do. The execution layer is unchanged. This tier requires no architectural change, only a swap of the perception module.

Empirical pedigree for the swap is recent. \citeonline{kirtac2024sentiment} compare BERT, OPT (a GPT-3-family decoder), FinBERT and the Loughran--McDonald dictionary on 965\,375 U.S.~financial news articles from January 2010 to June 2023, and report next-day return-prediction accuracies of 74.4\,\% for OPT, 72.5\,\% for BERT, 72.2\,\% for FinBERT and 50.1\,\% for Loughran--McDonald. The corresponding long--short portfolio Sharpe ratios are 3.05 for OPT, 2.11 for BERT and 2.07 for FinBERT. The evidence concerns U.S.~equities rather than Forex, and the Sharpe magnitudes should be read under the methodological caveats of Section~\ref{subsec:rule-significance} of the methodology chapter; nevertheless, the relative ordering OPT $>$ BERT $\approx$ FinBERT $\gg$ lexicon is the precise pedigree that motivates the Tier~A swap of the present work's scorer for a contemporary foundation model.

\subsection{Tier B: specialist multi-agent perception} \label{subsec:future-tier-b}

A more ambitious extension decomposes the perception layer into specialist agents, each responsible for a particular kind of source. Concrete candidates include a monetary-policy parser specialized on FOMC, ECB and BOJ communications; a geopolitical-event-extraction agent specialized on the GDELT Global Knowledge Graph; a macroeconomic-news classifier; and a chart-context describer that converts price-series segments into a textual summary consumed by the other agents. Each agent emits structured features that are concatenated into the news-derived input matrix of Section~\ref{sec:fusion}. The deterministic execution layer is unchanged; the agents merely produce a richer perception. Concurrent work outside the Forex domain explores closely related multi-agent decompositions: TradingGroup \cite{tian2025tradinggroup} introduces specialist agents for sentiment analysis, report interpretation, trend forecasting and style adaptation, with a self-reflection mechanism and an automated data-synthesis pipeline that generates post-training data for the agents, and QuantAgent \cite{xiong2025quantagent} factorises the technical-analysis layer of a high-frequency price-driven system into independent indicator, pattern, trend and risk agents. The present-work tier B differs in that the specialist agents feed cached features into the deterministic execution layer rather than emitting trade decisions directly, which preserves the validation discipline of \citeonline{lopezdeprado2018advances}. This tier introduces orchestration and caching concerns that the current LEAN runtime is not optimized for, and motivates the platform-migration discussion of Section~\ref{subsec:future-platform}.

\subsection{Tier C: consensus across heterogeneous agents} \label{subsec:future-tier-c}

A natural extension of Tier B is a consensus layer that aggregates the outputs of several agents using different methodological approaches over the same input. A sentiment score, for example, may be produced independently by a dictionary-based scorer following \citeonline{loughran2011liability}, by a fine-tuned BERT model in the FinBERT family \cite{araci2019finbert,yang2020finbert}, and by a foundation-model agent. Their three outputs are combined by a decision-tree-like aggregator into a single consensus score. The aggregator may be a fixed rule (majority vote, weighted average) or a learned one (a gradient-boosted tree on the agent outputs). Whichever it is, the consensus output is one more feature feeding the deterministic execution rule of Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution}; it does not become the trade decision. The methodological commitment of agentic perception with deterministic execution is preserved.

The advantage of consensus over single-agent perception is robustness: agreement among heterogeneous approaches is evidence of a real signal, while disagreement flags ambiguity and may translate, through the rule layer, into a HOLD. The disadvantage is computational cost (multiple model calls per article) and an enlarged surface area for prompt sensitivity and model drift. The cost is bounded by caching the agent outputs as Parquet artifacts, in line with the storage architecture of Section~\ref{sec:data-architecture}, so that the consensus is recomputed only when a new agent version is introduced.

The agentic-execution lineage of TradingAgents \cite{xiao2024tradingagents} on US equities explores a related but distinct variant, in which role-playing analyst agents reach a trade decision through structured debate rather than by feeding features into a downstream rule. The present design differs in that the consensus is a feature, not a decision, which preserves the validation discipline of \citeonline{lopezdeprado2018advances} that purely agentic-execution systems struggle to honor.

\subsection{Tier D: agent-proposed rule modifications} \label{subsec:future-tier-d}

The most ambitious tier turns the agents into proposers of rule modifications rather than producers of features. The deterministic execution rule of Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution} is treated as a parameterized template; an agent surveys recent backtest results, proposes a parameter change, and the change is gated by a walk-forward acceptance criterion before it is committed to the live rule layer. The execution layer remains deterministic at any point in time; the agents become rule-evolution proposers, not trade-decision makers. This tier reuses the configuration schema of the project's specifications, including the policy that distinguishes trading-impactful parameters from operational parameters, so that rule modifications are limited to the parameters the schema declares modifiable.

\subsection{Platform migration toward an actor-based engine} \label{subsec:future-platform}

The LEAN engine adopted in Section~\ref{sec:execution-engine} is well-suited to the deterministic execution model of Chapter~\ref{cap:methodology}. As the perception layer evolves toward Tiers~B, C and D, the orchestration of multiple specialist agents (with their independent caches, model calls and lifecycles) becomes a substantial engineering concern of its own. A natural target for the next-generation platform is an actor-based engine such as NautilusTrader\footnote{\url{https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader}}, whose architecture cleanly separates concurrent components and is used in production high-frequency settings. NautilusTrader's license is LGPL v3, which constrains commercial reuse more strictly than LEAN's Apache~2.0 license but remains compatible with academic and proprietary deployments that honor the license conditions.

The migration is not a goal of the present work. The deterministic execution rule, the configuration schema and the regression-test fixtures are, by design, engine-agnostic: they consume cached features and produce trade decisions without depending on any specific framework. Migrating the runtime therefore means re-implementing the engine-side wiring (subscriptions, indicator updates, order placement) against a different host while leaving the rule logic and the perception cache intact.

\subsection{From MVP to production: backtest, paper trading, live capital} \label{subsec:future-live}

The artifact targeted by the present work is a minimum viable product (MVP): a self-contained pipeline that ingests price and text data, computes features under the discipline of Section~\ref{sec:feature-layer}, drives the deterministic filter chain of Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution} and produces backtest results on EUR/USD (and, where the manuscript-freeze schedule allows it, USD/JPY) under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}. The portion of the MVP that has been brought to a runnable state at the time of submission is reported in Chapter~\ref{cap:experimental-evaluation}; portions deferred to post-publication continuation are listed explicitly in the same chapter. The MVP, in the portion that is runnable, is also the foundation for a three-stage transition to production-grade live trading. The architectural choices made in Chapter~\ref{cap:methodology} were taken with that transition in mind.

The first stage retargets the same strategy code against LEAN's OANDA brokerage adapter\footnote{The Apache 2.0 license on both LEAN and its OANDA adapter was a deciding factor in the choice of execution engine (Section~\ref{sec:execution-engine}). A copyleft-licensed engine would have constrained the post-publication continuation considered here.} in paper-trading mode. This requires the run-time loader of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility} to support live-data subscriptions in addition to historical Parquet replay. Both are within the reach of LEAN's existing API and require no modification to the rule layer. The second stage runs the same code against a funded OANDA demo account, with the deterministic execution layer untouched, in order to surface integration issues (latency, partial fills, weekend gaps, rollover handling) that the historical backtest does not expose. The third stage migrates to live capital with conservative position-sizing caps, audit-log retention and a kill switch wired into the risk-guard filter (Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution}). The same filter-chain configuration drives all three stages; the only changes between them are the data source, the brokerage adapter and the capital cap.

Two methodological commitments are intended to survive the transition. First, every trade (backtest, paper or live) carries the same filter-by-filter audit trail (Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic-execution}), so a forensic post-mortem of any live decision reproduces the reasoning end to end. Second, the perception cache of Section~\ref{sec:data-architecture} is the canonical record of every model output that influenced a decision, so a regression of the entire decision sequence is reproducible even after the underlying perception models drift or are upgraded. Whether the MVP fulfills these commitments in practice is the subject of the empirical work reported in Chapter~\ref{cap:experimental-evaluation} and the work program of the post-publication roadmap above.

\subsection{Knowledge-graph mediation and unsupervised news anomaly features} \label{subsec:future-kg-anomaly}

Two perception-layer extensions are recorded here as future work because they introduce new infrastructure rather than swap or refine an existing component. The first is a knowledge-graph-mediation pattern in the sense of \citeonline{brito2025grafos}: in place of feeding raw GDELT or news text into the LLM scorer, the ingestion pipeline materializes a structured graph (actors, locations, themes, emotional indices, Goldstein scale) and the LLM is prompted against the graph slice relevant to the pair under evaluation. The benefits are auditability of the context delivered to the model, deterministic reconstruction of the prompt at any timestamp, and a smaller per-call payload. The graph layer can be implemented either on a dedicated store (Neo4j, RedisGraph) or directly over the existing Parquet warehouse using DuckDB's PGQ extension; the choice affects only the ingestion path and not the deterministic filter chain.

The second extension is an unsupervised anomaly-detection feature over the news stream itself, inspired by the LogBERT-style sequence-anomaly methodology of \citeonline{santos2024infra}. Rather than scoring article-level polarity, the layer flags timestamps at which the topical or volumetric distribution of recent headlines for a given currency departs from its rolling baseline. The output is a non-directional risk-regime feature that complements the directional sentiment channel. The deterministic execution layer already accepts non-directional features at the news-context filter stage, so the integration cost is bounded.

\subsection{Strategy-variant ablations} \label{subsec:future-ablations}

The methodology of Chapter~\ref{cap:methodology} implements one strategy variant and evaluates it on EUR/USD and USD/JPY. A natural extension is a family of sibling variants that share the same filter chain but differ in the reward-to-risk ratio and in the trailing-stop policy. Adding each variant as an additional YAML configuration, using the inheritance semantics of the filter-chain configuration schema, and running the full evaluation pipeline against each produces an ablation study with no further methodological change. The ablation is a self-contained extension of the present work and is the most natural first follow-up.

\subsection{Application to other financial markets} \label{subsec:future-multiasset}

The architecture described in Chapter~\ref{cap:methodology}, agentic perception with deterministic execution via a configurable filter chain, is not specific to Forex. The empirical evaluation of the present work is conducted exclusively on the foreign-exchange market (EUR/USD and USD/JPY), and that is the deliberate scope of the dissertation; the design, however, is asset-class agnostic in principle and was constructed so that adapting it to other markets is an exercise in configuration and adapter substitution rather than redesign. The market-varying constructs are isolated behind two seams. First, the tradable instrument is modeled by a common value object carrying the attributes shared by any market (symbol, quoting precision, the named price-movement unit and its size, minimum tradable size) composed with an asset-class-specific component that supplies only what is particular to that class; the Forex case populates a currency-pair component (base and quote currencies, the pip as the price-movement unit), whereas an equity, ETF, bond, futures or cryptocurrency case would populate its own component (exchange, contract specification, settlement convention) without altering the common object or its consumers. Second, the constructs that vary operationally (quoting conventions, position sizing, session calendar, margin and leverage) are encapsulated in dedicated filters (capital-management, risk-guard) and parameterized in the configuration, not hard-coded. The perception layer, the late-fusion meta-learner, the audit trail and the configuration policy carry no market-specific assumption. Should the evaluation reported in Chapter~\ref{cap:experimental-evaluation} demonstrate measurable value on Forex, the same methodology applies to equities, ETFs, bonds, futures, commodities or cryptocurrencies (and, more generally, to financial markets at large) by swapping the bulk-download adapter (analogous to the Dukascopy adapter) and the broker adapter (LEAN exposes adapters for Interactive Brokers, Alpaca, Coinbase and others) while keeping the perception, fusion and execution layers structurally unchanged. This generalization is contingent on the Forex evaluation succeeding; it is recorded here as a path for future research, not as a parallel commitment of the present work.
